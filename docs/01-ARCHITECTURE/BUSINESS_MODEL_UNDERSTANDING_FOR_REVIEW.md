## 0. The Golden Rule — Business Decision vs User Experience

The system may calculate internally using the reservation engine. It may know — internally — the physical inventory, the reserved quantity, the remaining capacity, the expected executable quantity, the first-come-first-served priority, and the automatic allocation. **These calculations are internal.**

**The user experience must NOT expose the internal calculation model.** Every screen must answer only the business question the user currently has — nothing more.

- **While adding to cart** — the user's question is: *"Can I add this quantity?"* The answer is **YES** or **NO**. If NO, show the maximum quantity allowed for this invoice using the smart quantity formatter. Do not explain reservation logic here.
- **While submitting an order** — the user's question is: *"Will my order be accepted?"* The answer is **YES**. If previous reservations exist, show a business notice only — explain what MAY happen later, never how the engine works.
- **While approving** — the user's question is: *"What quantity was finally approved?"* If an automatic adjustment happened, show the business reason — e.g. *"The quantity was automatically adjusted because previous submitted invoices had priority."* Do not explain FCFS. Do not explain allocation. Explain the business outcome.

**The Golden Rule, stated once and applied everywhere:**

> **The system may calculate using Reservation. The system must communicate using Business.**

> Users should never feel they are interacting with a reservation engine. They should feel they are interacting with an intelligent inventory system.

This principle governs every screen and every message in this document (sections 9, 10, 12) and overrides any wording that would leak the internal model.

## 1. What is Physical Inventory?

Physical Inventory is **the goods actually sitting in the warehouse for a product**, counted in pieces (single units). It is one number per product: "how many pieces of this product do we physically have right now."

It is the **source of truth for what we can actually sell and deliver today**. It is the ONLY basis for blocking a request (section 6). It goes down only when a real event happens (an order is approved and its goods are committed), and it goes back up when an approved order is undone (cancelled or edited).

The physical stock is invisible to sales representatives. They only ever see availability guidance in business units (sections 6, 7, 11).

---

## 2. What is Reservation?

Reservation is **an accounting memory** — nothing more.

It exists for one purpose: **so the system remembers that previous invoices have already requested quantities** of a product, and can use that memory later, at approval, to allocate the stock fairly.

Three absolute principles:

1. **Reservation is a memory, not a limiter.** It remembers what was requested. It does not reduce physical stock, and it does not reduce what a new customer may request.
2. **Reservation must NEVER be the reason business is prevented.** It can never reject an order, never block a cart, never force a quantity down before approval.
3. **Reservation is an information system, not a rejection system.** It informs the allocation engine at approval — nothing before that.

How the memory works:

- A reservation is simply *the current content of a submitted order*, read at any moment. It can never be out of date: whatever the order says right now *is* its memory. If the order is changed, the memory changes with it automatically.
- It is **per product inside the order**, not just per order. Removing one product releases that product's memory only.
- It is kept in **whole selling units** (full cartons, full dozens, full pieces) — never fractions.
- It **never expires** by time. It is released only by an order event: the order leaves "submitted" (approval, cancellation, or any status change), or an edit changes/removes content.
- It exists **only while the order is submitted**. Drafts are not remembered; approved orders are no longer "remembered" as reserved — they are consumed.

**The key mental model:** stock = 100, submitted order A asks for 80 → the memory records "reserved = 80", but the remaining physical stock is **still 100**. Another customer may freely request 70 — nothing stops them.

---

## 3. What is Final Allocation?

Final Allocation is **the moment, at approval, when the system decides exactly how many pieces of a product each competing order will actually get.**

It uses the reservation memory. It matters when several submitted orders together ask for more than the physical stock.

The rule is **First Come, First Served (FCFS) by submission time**:

1. Submitted orders for the same product are lined up by who submitted first.
2. The earliest submitted order gets its full requested quantity first.
3. The next order gets whatever is left — and so on.
4. An order that cannot get everything is **automatically reduced** to the largest whole selling units that remain (full cartons/dozens, then pieces). No fractions.

**The reservation engine becomes authoritative ONLY at approval.** Before approval it only remembers; at approval it decides. Allocation, automatic adjustment, and deduction all happen in one approval action.

When the company allows orders to exceed current stock (over-stock selling), there is no competition — every order gets exactly what it asked for, and allocation is a formality.

**This allocation logic is the single calculation engine (Business Contract rule 1).** The expected executable quantity shown in the submit notice is computed by exactly this same logic — never a second, "display-only" calculation. If nothing changes in the system, the quantity told to the user before approval is the quantity that will actually be approved.

---

## 4. When does Reservation start?

**Reservation starts the moment an order is submitted** — the moment the order leaves "draft" and becomes a submitted order. From that moment the system remembers its content for the product.

- **Draft order (not yet sent):** no reservation memory. Not a commitment, not in any queue.
- **Submitted order:** the memory records its content, and it takes its place in the first-come-first-served queue by submission time.
- **Approved order:** the memory for it ends — it is now consumed, not remembered.
- **Any other status:** no reservation memory ever. If an order leaves "submitted" for any reason, its memory is released automatically.

None of this ever blocks anything (section 7).

---

## 5. When does Inventory actually decrease?

**Physical inventory decreases only at approval** — specifically, when an approved order reaches its configured point of deduction (by default, the moment it is approved). At that point the allocated quantity is actually subtracted from the physical stock, and the reservation memory for that order ends.

Before approval nothing physical changes. Submitting, being remembered, being informed — none of these touch the warehouse number. Only approval causes the actual deduction.

The reverse is equally true: if an approved (already deducted) order is later **cancelled or edited**, the deducted quantity is automatically returned to the physical stock, and the order's new content (if any) is re-synced.

## 6. When should the system block adding to the cart?

**The cart has exactly ONE blocking rule:**

> **Block ONLY when the requested quantity exceeds the PHYSICAL INVENTORY.**

Example:
- Physical inventory = **3 Cartons + 270 Pieces**
- Customer requests = **4 Cartons** → **reject immediately**.
- Display: **"Maximum quantity allowed for this invoice — 📦 3 Cartons + 270 Pieces"**.

This is a **stock limitation** — the only kind of block that exists.

Consequences of this rule:

- A product with zero physical stock is unavailable — that is the same rule with a zero maximum.
- **Reservations never block the cart.** The fact that other submitted orders have requested quantities is never a reason to block, reduce, or reject anything a new customer requests. The physical stock is the only limit.

The blocking message is presented as the **live RED business status** directly below the Buy button, refreshed instantly on every quantity change (section 0B, Case 3).

## 7. When should the system only display a warning?

The submission notice is **not a static sentence**. It is a **dynamic business message generated by rule at the moment of submission**, according to the current scenario. Exactly one family of situations triggers it: when earlier submitted invoices already hold a reservation memory for the same product.

**The three submission rules (official contract):**

1. **Requested quantity exceeds PHYSICAL inventory** → the request is **rejected at the cart** (it never reaches submission). The user sees only: *"الحد الأقصى المسموح به في هذه الفاتورة هو: 📦 X كرتونة + Y قطعة"* — the smart maximum allowed (sections 6 and 11). Nothing else is shown.

2. **Requested quantity is within PHYSICAL inventory but earlier submitted invoices already hold a reservation for the product** → the order is **accepted, never rejected**. The customer is shown a business notice that answers the only question that matters — *"What does this mean for my order?"* — and nothing more. The notice:
   - **Never mentions Reservation.**
   - **Never mentions FCFS.**
   - **Never mentions Allocation.**
   - **Never mentions any internal calculation.**
   - Explains only the **business impact**, and always states the **expected executable quantity** — what the customer can realistically expect to receive if the earlier orders are approved first — as a **dynamically calculated smart quantity** (section 11).

3. **No earlier submitted invoices hold a reservation for the product** → **no message is shown at all.** Silent acceptance.

The expected executable quantity is calculated internally using the same logic that decides the final share at approval (section 3); the user is shown only the resulting number, in smart business units — never the calculation, never the engine.

**The expected executable quantity is NOT the remaining physical inventory.** It is the quantity expected to be executable if all previously submitted invoices are approved before the current invoice — the current invoice's expected share, produced by the same allocation calculation that will be used at approval (Business Contract rules 1 and 3).

The example business wording (the quantity is inserted dynamically) — **the same wording used by the quantity-selection YELLOW status** (section 0B, Case 2):

> 🟡 سيتم قبول طلبك.
> يوجد طلبات سابقة لهذا الصنف لم يتم اعتمادها بعد.
> إذا تم اعتماد الطلبات السابقة أولاً،
> فسيكون المتاح لتنفيذ طلبك:
> 📦 X كرتونة + Y قطعة

If the accumulation grows, the same notice states the new expectation (for example: *"...فسيكون المتاح لتنفيذ طلبك: 0 قطعة"*).

**Summary of the rule:** the cart blocks only against physical inventory (section 6); submission never rejects for any reservation reason; the submission notice is dynamic — it appears only when earlier submitted invoices exist, and it states only the business impact with a dynamically calculated executable quantity.

## 8. What is the exact difference between Reservation and Inventory?

| | Physical Inventory | Reservation |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | The goods physically in the warehouse for a product (in pieces) | An accounting memory of what submitted invoices have requested |
| **Is it real?** | Yes — actual goods | No — a remembered count of requests |
| **What can it block?** | It is the ONLY thing that can block the cart (section 6) | **It blocks nothing, ever** |
| **What is it for?** | Know what we actually have and can deliver | Remember requests so the allocation engine can be fair at approval |
| **When does it change?** | Only at approval (deduction) or on undo of an approved order (restore) | Every time a submitted order is created, edited, removed, or changes status |
| **Seen by sales reps?** | No — only guidance | No — only guidance |
| **Seen by management?** | Yes, per permissions | Yes, per permissions (order details, action history) |

One sentence: **Physical inventory is the real stock and the only thing that can ever refuse a quantity; reservation is just the system's memory of what earlier submitted invoices asked for — it informs the approval-time allocation and never prevents any business.**

## 9. The complete customer journey

Throughout the journey, the Golden Rule applies: **the system calculates internally with Reservation, but communicates using Business.** Each stage answers exactly one question — the question the user has at that moment — and nothing more.

**Add to Cart (quantity selection)**
The customer's question: *"What will happen to the quantity I selected?"* → Answer: a **live business status** directly below the Buy button, refreshed on every quantity change (section 0B):
- **GREEN** — the selected quantity can be fulfilled completely: *"سيتم تنفيذ هذه الكمية بالكامل."*
- **YELLOW** — the selected quantity can be added (it exists physically), but earlier submitted invoices exist for the product: the order is accepted, never blocked, and the expected executable quantity is stated — *"سيتم قبول طلبك... فسيكون المتاح لتنفيذ طلبك: 📦 X كرتونة + Y قطعة"*.
- **RED** — the selected quantity exceeds the physical stock: **cannot be added**, showing the maximum allowed in smart units.
The system compares the request against the **physical inventory** only for blocking; reservations never block — they only inform the YELLOW status.

**Submit Order**
The customer's question: *"Will my order be accepted?"* → Answer: **YES**.
The customer sends the order. The order is **always accepted**. Two things happen:
- The order becomes *submitted* and its content is added to the **reservation memory** for each product.
- It takes its place in the **first-come-first-served queue** by submission time.

If earlier submitted invoices already hold a reservation for the same product, the customer is shown the **dynamic business notice** (section 7, rule 2): the order is accepted, and if the earlier orders are approved first, the **expected executable quantity** is stated as a dynamically calculated smart quantity (possibly zero). If no earlier submitted invoices hold a reservation, **no notice is shown** (section 7, rule 3). The customer is never refused. **The notice explains what MAY happen later — it never explains how the engine works and never mentions Reservation, FCFS, or Allocation.**

**Reservation**
From submission onward, the order's content *is* its remembered reservation. If the customer edits the order, the memory follows automatically — up, down, or released for removed products — and the change is recorded in the order's action history. The customer never sees "the memory" — only the order's content and quantities in business units.

**Approval**
Management's question: *"What quantity was finally approved?"* → Answer: the approved quantity, and — if it was automatically adjusted — the business reason.
Management approves the order. **Now, and only now, the reservation engine becomes authoritative.** In one approval action:
- **FCFS** — orders are served by submission time,
- **Allocation** — each order's actual share is decided from the memory,
- **Automatic quantity adjustment** — any order that cannot be fully covered is reduced to whole selling units,
- **Inventory deduction** — the final allocated quantity is subtracted from the physical stock, and the order's reservation memory ends.

The manager is shown the business outcome — the approved quantity and, if adjusted, the business reason (e.g. "the quantity was automatically adjusted because previous submitted invoices had priority"). **FCFS, allocation, and the engine itself are never shown or named to the user.**

**Inventory Deduction**
The physical stock now reflects reality. If the approved order is later cancelled or edited, the deducted quantity is returned to stock automatically and the order's new state is re-synced.

## 11. The smart quantity formatter and where it must be used

**What it is:** A formatting rule that turns a number of pieces into the most natural wholesale description — **whole cartons + remaining pieces** (or **whole dozens + remaining pieces**), always preferring the selling unit the customer chose.

Examples (as the business specified):

- 📦 3 Cartons + 270 Pieces
- 📚 22 Dozens + 6 Pieces
- 🧴 270 Pieces

Additional examples:
- 1080 pieces, carton = 270 → "4 كرتونة" (4 cartons)
- 295 pieces, carton = 270 → "1 كرتونة + 25 قطعة" (1 carton + 25 pieces)

**The rule:** when talking about a **maximum allowed quantity** or an **expected/executable quantity**, **raw pieces are never displayed** — the smart quantity is always used. This applies to the cart maximum message and to the reservation notice's expected executable quantity.

**Where it must be used — everywhere a quantity is shown to a business user:**

1. The **cart maximum message** (section 10-A1).
2. The **expected executable quantity** inside the reservation notice (section 10-B3).
3. **Quantities on the Order Details screen** (products in cartons + pieces / dozens + pieces; statuses referencing quantities).
4. Any future message that must present an amount to a customer or rep in their chosen unit.

It is **never** used for internal/technical numbers; it is purely a presentation rule for humans.

**(Business Contract rule 4:** every quantity shown to a user must use this formatter on every screen. **Rule 5:** this formatter is the single source of all quantity wording in the system — no screen formats its own quantities.)

## 12. The Order Details screen from a business user's perspective

From a manager's point of view, the Order Details screen is **the complete story of one order** — what was ordered, what it means for the stock, and what happened to it and why.

It shows:

1. **The invoice itself:** invoice number, customer, dates, order status (in business words), and notes.
2. **The products:** every item with its quantity in **wholesale language** (e.g. "2 كرتونة + 40 قطعة"), unit price, and line total.
3. **The money:** subtotal, discount, and final total.
4. **Inventory position per product (management only):** the ordered quantity, the quantity remembered as reserved for this order, the physical stock, and the product's reservation state — labeled in business terms. No raw internal codes.
5. **The action history (the heart of it):** a chronological, business-language log of everything that touched this order's quantities:
   - the quantity was remembered as reserved when the order was submitted,
   - the reservation memory was updated when the order was edited,
   - a previous-reservation notice was issued at submission (with the expected executable quantity),
   - the quantity was automatically reduced at approval (with the resulting quantity),
   - the reservation was released, the quantity was deducted, or — on cancel/edit — the quantity was returned to stock.
   - Every entry shows **who did it, when, why (in Arabic), and the quantity before and after.**

The manager can, from this one screen, answer any question a customer might ask: "Why did I only get 3 cartons instead of 4?" → because earlier invoices had already requested the stock, and the allocation was automatic and fair by submission order. That story is right there on the screen, in business language.

Throughout this screen, the Golden Rule holds: the manager sees the **business story** of the order — quantities in wholesale language, and reasons in business words. The underlying engine calculations are never exposed; the screen answers the manager's current question about the order.

## Closing — my understanding in one paragraph

**Physical inventory** is the real warehouse stock and the **only thing that can ever refuse a quantity** (only at the cart, and only when the request exceeds physical stock — showing the smart maximum allowed). **Reservation is an accounting memory** of what submitted invoices have requested; it never blocks, never rejects, never reduces anything before approval — it exists solely to inform the allocation engine. An order is **drafted** (nothing remembered) → **submitted** (accepted always; its content joins the memory; a business notice tells the customer the expected executable quantity if earlier invoices exist; it takes its place in the first-come-first-served queue) → **approved** (here — and only here — the engine becomes authoritative: FCFS → allocation → automatic quantity adjustment → inventory deduction) → **delivered**. Every screen answers only the business question the user currently has, every message answers "what does this mean for my order?" in business Arabic, and every quantity shown to a person is the smart quantity (cartons + pieces), never raw pieces. **The system may calculate using Reservation; the system must communicate using Business.**
